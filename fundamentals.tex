\section{Fundamentals}
The properties of the ultimate display follow from human vision. Before discussing display technology, it is therefore necessary to understand exactly why a two-dimensional image of an object looks less convincing than the real thing.
\subsection{Depth perception}
Depth perception is typically analyzed in terms of various contributing factors called depth cues.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{l p{2.5cm} p{2.5cm}}
 & \textbf{Monocular} & \textbf{Binocular} \\ 
 \hline
 \textbf{Visual} & Pictorial cues & Retinal disparity \\  
 \hline
 \textbf{Oculomotor} & Accomodation & Convergence \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For the purpose of this discussion, depth cues will be divided into four groups according to two criteria. Visual cues arise from the visual stimuli themselves, while oculomotor cues are sensed by proprioceptors in the eye muscles as they work to optimize these stimuli. Binocular cues are extracted from differences between the views seen by the left and the right eye, while monocular cues are inferred from a single view. This grouping of depth cues is useful because most display technologies that will be discussed later provide either none or all of the cues within each group. Visual monocular cues are pictorial cues such as occlusion, linear perspective, texture, shading, and prior knowledge. They are called pictorial cues because they can be conveyed by conventional 2D displays, paintings or in print. Retinal disparity, a visual binocular cue arises from the slight difference in perspective between the left and the right eye. Light originating from the same point in space does not necessarily reach corresponding points on the left and right retinas. A detailed explanation of the geometry is given by Patterson and Martin (1992). \cite{patterson1992human} Accomodation and pupillary response are oculomotor monocular cues. Accomodation refers to subconscious adjustments made to the optical power of the eyes to obtain a clear view. How much the eye lenses must be stretched to obtain focus depends on the distance of the viewed object. Similarly, the pupil dilates or constricts in response to changes in brightness
